<p class="home-intro">
Hi, I'm a Ph.D. student at the <a href="https://natur.cuni.cz/en">Faculty of Science</a>, <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University">Charles University</a> in Prague. I work in the <a href="https://www.heu.cas.cz/department/department-of-theoretical-chemistry#department">Department of Theoretical Chemistry</a> of the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaroslav_Heyrovsk%C3%BD">Heyrovský</a> Institute, <a href="https://www.avcr.cz/en/">Czech Academy of Sciences</a>, as a member of <a href="https://www.heu.cas.cz/~pittner/">Jiří Pittner's group</a>.
</p>

<p class="home-intro">
My research focuses on machine learning for nonadiabatic molecular dynamics. I develop ML models for excited-state potential energy surfaces and the couplings between them, which make surface hopping simulations of photochemical processes faster and allow them to reach longer timescales.
</p>

<section class="home-section">
  <h2>Research</h2>
  <p>
    Our group studies <em>ultrafast dynamics of molecules and clusters including nonadiabatic and spin–orbit effects</em>. If you would like to learn more about our research or my <a href="{{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}">projects</a>, feel free to reach out!
  </p>
  <div class="home-cards">
    <div class="home-card">
      <i class="fa-solid fa-brain"></i>
      <h3>Machine learning for excited states</h3>
      <p>Models for potential energy surfaces, nonadiabatic and spin–orbit couplings that make long simulations affordable.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="home-card">
      <i class="fa-solid fa-atom"></i>
      <h3>Nonadiabatic dynamics</h3>
      <p>Surface hopping simulations of what molecules do after absorbing light, including intersystem crossing.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="home-card">
      <i class="fa-solid fa-code"></i>
      <h3>Software</h3>
      <p>We contribute to the <a href="https://newtonx.org/">Newton-X</a> and <a href="http://mlatom.com/">MLatom</a> packages.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <p class="home-note">
    Other topics of our group include quantum chemical methods for strongly correlated systems (multireference and DMRG-based externally corrected coupled cluster methods) and quantum computer algorithms for quantum chemistry.
  </p>
</section>
